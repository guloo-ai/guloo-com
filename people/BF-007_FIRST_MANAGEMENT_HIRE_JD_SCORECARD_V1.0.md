## 1. Recommended role

**Title:** COO / Chairman Office Lead（營運長／董事長辦公室主管）

This is the first management-layer human hire for the GULOO AI-native operating model. The role is designed to stabilize daily company execution, turn Chairman direction into owned operating cadence, and make human operations legible to AI Agents.

This role is not a ceremonial strategy position. It must be close to the field, numbers, people, exceptions and execution.

## 2. Mission

Build a dependable human operating layer around the Chairman and YONGGUANG-AGENT so GULOO can scale without becoming operationally chaotic.

The role must:
- translate strategic priorities into accountable work;
- keep daily operations stable;
- close cross-functional execution gaps;
- maintain clear owners, SOPs, data and exception handling;
- make company operations machine-readable and auditable for AI-native coordination;
- protect people, quality, cash, customer trust and operational continuity.

## 3. Core responsibilities

### A. Chairman Office / Operating cadence
- Maintain weekly and daily execution priorities.
- Convert Chairman decisions into bounded tasks with owners and deadlines.
- Surface only red/yellow exceptions that require executive judgment.
- Ensure unresolved blockers have a named owner and next action.
- Maintain decision and execution traceability.

### B. Operations stabilization
- Coordinate purchasing, inventory, product operations, store/food-service operations, scheduling, quality, cost and abnormal-event handling.
- Ensure daily operational problems are resolved at the correct level instead of repeatedly escalating to the Chairman.
- Drive root-cause correction, not only temporary firefighting.

### C. Supply chain coordination
- Establish clear source -> owner -> rule -> execution -> verification loops for procurement, material availability, production/assembly, stock and fulfillment.
- Coordinate safety stock, stock accuracy and replenishment discipline.
- Ensure exceptions such as shortages, quality failures and late delivery are visible early.

### D. SOP and data discipline
- Standardize the minimum SOPs needed for reliable operations.
- Ensure key data fields have owners and sources of truth.
- Work with Product/Data, Finance and YONGGUANG-AGENT to improve master-data quality.
- Prevent "everyone has a different spreadsheet" operating patterns.

### E. AI-native human operating interface
- Treat AI Agents as governed operating partners, not uncontrolled automation.
- Provide structured facts, owners and approval boundaries that Agents can understand.
- Review Agent-generated recommendations and exception summaries where human judgment is needed.
- Never bypass Constitution, Permission, Approval, Budget or Audit gates.

### F. People and accountability
- Clarify role ownership and expected outcomes.
- Build a culture of clear commitments, early escalation and respectful execution.
- Coach teams to use data, SOPs and post-mortem learning rather than blame.
- Protect human dignity and workload sustainability while improving execution quality.

## 4. Decision authority

### May decide within delegated authority
- daily prioritization within approved goals;
- task assignment and follow-up;
- SOP improvement proposals and reversible internal process improvements;
- non-financial operational coordination;
- escalation timing and incident coordination;
- data-quality follow-up and ownership clarification.

### Requires Chairman / authorized approval
- hiring/firing beyond delegated authority;
- compensation or material budget changes;
- binding contracts;
- major supplier/customer commercial commitments;
- pricing exceptions below governed floors;
- bank/payment/credential/access changes;
- legal commitments;
- destructive or irreversible production changes.

## 5. Candidate profile

### Must have
- strong operational judgment;
- comfort with numbers and operating metrics;
- strong process thinking and field sense;
- ability to handle ambiguity and incidents calmly;
- habit of closing loops and documenting ownership;
- ability to work directly with a founder/chairman;
- willingness to adopt AI-native workflows without over-automating people.

### Strongly preferred
- operations / supply chain / food service / retail / manufacturing background;
- ERP or inventory-system experience;
- multi-site operating experience;
- experience building SOPs and management cadence from an early-stage environment;
- experience coordinating finance, product, purchasing and front-line operations.

### Not suitable if
- primarily presentation-oriented with weak operating follow-through;
- avoids field problems;
- depends on the Chairman to resolve routine exceptions;
- treats data governance as clerical work;
- resists auditability or clear accountability;
- sees AI only as a novelty or as a substitute for judgment.

## 7. First 30 days — Understand and stabilize

### Outcomes
1. Produce a current-state operating map covering:
   - purchasing;
   - inventory;
   - product/SKU;
   - stores/service operations;
   - scheduling;
   - quality;
   - cost;
   - exception handling;
   - ERP/data ownership.
2. Identify top 10 recurring operational failure modes.
3. Assign accountable owners to P0/P1 operating processes.
4. Establish daily/weekly operating cadence.
5. Create a Chairman red-light summary format.
6. Verify that critical stock, cost and product data have named source systems and owners.

### 30-day scorecard
| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| Critical operating processes mapped | >= 90% of P0 scope |
| P0/P1 processes with named owner | 100% |
| Top recurring exceptions identified | Top 10 documented |
| Weekly operating review cadence | Running consistently |
| Critical data-source ambiguity | Material gaps documented and assigned |
| Chairman routine operational interruptions | Baseline established |

## 8. Days 31–60 — Standardize and close loops

### Outcomes
1. Implement minimum SOPs for the highest-risk recurring workflows.
2. Establish exception categories, escalation thresholds and response owners.
3. Improve stock accuracy / replenishment visibility for priority items.
4. Connect Product Master and unit-economics governance into operating review.
5. Reduce repeated manual reporting through structured Agent-readable records.
6. Introduce post-incident corrective-action tracking.

### 60-day scorecard
| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| Priority recurring workflows with minimum SOP | >= 80% |
| P0 exceptions with explicit escalation rule | 100% |
| Open P0 blocker without owner | 0 |
| Critical SKU data-quality exceptions | Trending down week over week |
| Repeated incidents with root-cause action | >= 90% |
| Executive reporting | Red/yellow exception focused |

## 9. Days 61–90 — Build scalable operating system

### Outcomes
1. Demonstrate that routine operations can run without continuous Chairman intervention.
2. Establish a reliable operating dashboard / command-center feed.
3. Create 90-day improvement roadmap for supply chain, operations, data and people.
4. Define staffing gaps that truly require additional humans versus Agent/process automation.
5. Show measurable improvement in at least three operating health indicators.
6. Establish management succession/delegation discipline so execution does not depend on one individual.

### 90-day scorecard
| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| Routine P0 operations with clear owners and SOP | >= 90% |
| P0 unresolved blocker > agreed SLA | 0 without explicit escalation |
| Chairman time spent on routine operational coordination | Materially reduced vs 30-day baseline |
| Critical data fields with source + owner | >= 95% |
| Priority operating incidents with audit trail | 100% |
| Measurable operating indicators improved | >= 3 |
| Next-quarter operating roadmap | Approved/review-ready |

## 11. Failure signals in first 90 days

Escalate fit concerns if the hire consistently:
- reports activity rather than outcomes;
- hides problems until late;
- cannot name owner/source/next action;
- creates meetings instead of operating clarity;
- centralizes all decisions personally;
- ignores front-line reality;
- tolerates recurring stock/cost/data inconsistencies;
- bypasses governance for speed;
- increases human administrative burden without improving service or reliability.

## 12. AI-native operating principles for this role

The hire should use YONGGUANG-AGENT to reduce coordination burden, but must preserve human accountability.

Expected pattern:
**Human judgment + structured data + governed Agent execution + verification + audit trail.**

The role should progressively move humans away from repetitive administrative coordination and toward judgment, care, relationships, creativity and exception handling.

## 13. Hiring evaluation framework

Interview evidence should test:
- a real operational crisis they owned end-to-end;
- how they managed inventory/cost/process ambiguity;
- how they built SOPs without over-bureaucratizing;
- how they handled an underperforming owner/team member;
- how they prioritize when everything appears urgent;
- how they would work with AI Agents while preserving authority boundaries;
- how they protect people and quality under growth pressure.

A case exercise should ask the candidate to stabilize a hypothetical week involving:
- stock shortage;
- supplier delay;
- store/service complaint;
- cost variance;
- missing ERP data;
- Chairman priority change.

Assessment should focus on sequencing, ownership, data needed, escalation thresholds and closure—not presentation style.
